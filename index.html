Formula sheet

Topic 1

Opportunity cost on a PPF
OC(x) = units of y given up / 1 unit of x = |slope|
OCs of the two goods are reciprocals: OC(y) = 1 / OC(x).
Linear PPF from time/resources
a·X + b·Y = Total → Y = Total/b − (a/b)·X
HAL26: 3M + 12P = 120.
Joint PPF
Start at max of the vertical good. Move the producer with the lowest OC of the horizontal good first → kink → then the other.
HHI
HHI = Σ sᵢ² (shares in %)
0 → 10,000. Minimum with n firms = 10,000/n (2,500 for 4; 3,333 for 3).
Compound growth (Seminar 1)
R₅ = R₀·(1 + g)⁵

Topic 2

Price elasticity of demand
ε = %ΔQ / %ΔP = (ΔQ/ΔP)·(P₀/Q₀)
Use initial values. Point form: ε = Q'(p)·p/Q.
Linear demand Q = a − bp
ε = −bp/(a − bp); max revenue at p = a/2b (ε = −1)
Revenue after a price change
R₁/R₀ = (1 + %ΔP)(1 + %ΔQ)
Works backwards too: revenue −2%, price +10% → %ΔQ = 0.98/1.1 − 1.
Cross & income elasticity
ε_XY = %ΔQ_X / %ΔP_Y ε_R = %ΔQ / %ΔIncome
XED > 0 substitutes, < 0 complements. YED > 1 luxury, 0–1 necessity, < 0 inferior.
Combined effects
%ΔQ_X = ε_own·%ΔP_X + ε_cross·%ΔP_Y
Surplus (linear)
CS = ½(p_choke − p*)·Q* PS = ½(p* − p_min)·Q*
If supply has positive Q at p = 0 (e.g. Qs = 100 + 6p), PS is a trapezoid: ½(Qs(0) + Q*)·p*.

Topic 3 – price controls in four lines

Ceiling below p*
Traded Q = Qs. CS = rectangle (WTP at Qs − p_max)·Qs + triangle above it. PS = triangle under p_max. DWL = ½(WTP − p_max)(Q* − Qs).
Floor above p*
Traded Q = Qd. CS = triangle above p_min. PS = rectangle (p_min − supply price at Qd)·Qd + triangle below it.
Quota below Q*
Price from the demand curve at the quota. Same areas as a floor at that price.
Minimum wage
Job destruction = L* − Ld. Unemployment = Ls − Ld.

Seminar 3, worked

There is no official answer key for this one yet, so here is a full solution in the same style as the Seminar 2 key. Try it first.

Q1 · Housing: Qd = 60,000 − 20p, Qs = −20,000 + 30p

a) 60,000 − 20p = −20,000 + 30p → 80,000 = 50p → p* = €1,600, Q* = 28,000.
Demand hits p at 3,000; supply starts at p = 20,000/30 = 666.67.
CS = ½(3,000 − 1,600)(28,000) = €19.6m
PS = ½(1,600 − 666.67)(28,000) = €13.07m
TS = €32.67m

b) Ceiling €1,000: Qs = 10,000; Qd = 40,000 → shortage 30,000. WTP at Q = 10,000: p = 2,500.
CS = (2,500 − 1,000)(10,000) + ½(500)(10,000) = €17.5m
PS = ½(1,000 − 666.67)(10,000) = €1.67m
DWL = ½(2,500 − 1,000)(18,000) = €13.5m. Yes, there is a deadweight loss.

c) More inelastic supply → landlords withdraw fewer flats when the price falls → the quantity loss is smaller and the lower rent dominates → more beneficial for families. Here supply is quite elastic and renters as a group actually lose (19.6m → 17.5m).

d) Yes. Only 10,000 flats are available, and the marginal renter would pay up to €2,500 for one. That gap creates side payments, key money and black-market sub-letting.

e) Lower expected returns → less investment in new housing and less maintenance → lower quality over time; supply falls further in the long run (long-run supply is more elastic).

Q2 · Delivery workers: Ld = 6,000 − 4w, Ls = 2,000 + w

a) 6,000 − 4w = 2,000 + w → w* = €800, L* = 5,200.

b) At €1,000: Ld = 2,000, Ls = 3,000 → unemployment = 1,000 (job destruction = 5,200 − 2,000 = 3,200).

c) Mixed. The 2,000 who keep their job earn €200 more; 3,200 lose their job. Worker surplus as a group falls from about €2.88m to €2.0m (assuming the jobs go to those with the lowest reservation wage). Firms lose too (€1.82m → €0.5m).

d) More elastic labour demand → more job destruction. More elastic demand and supply → more unemployment (supply elasticity adds the extra people who want to work at the higher wage).

Q3 · Apples: Qd = 200 − 2p, Qs = 3p

a) 200 − 2p = 3p → p* = 40, Q* = 120. CS = ½(100 − 40)(120) = 3,600; PS = ½(40)(120) = 2,400; TS = 6,000.

b) Quota 80: price from demand, 80 = 200 − 2p → p = 60. Supply price at 80 is 80/3 = 26.67.
PS = (60 − 26.67)(80) + ½(26.67)(80) = 3,733 (up from 2,400). CS = ½(40)(80) = 1,600. DWL = ½(60 − 26.67)(40) = 667.

c) The more inelastic demand, the more the price jumps when quantity is cut, so producers gain. With elastic demand the price barely rises and they lose from selling less.

Mock exam

New questions in the style of the past papers. Write your answer on paper, then open the solution.

1Short-term rental prices in Barcelona rise sharply. What happens to the opportunity cost of a flat owner who rents long-term to students? Explain.[4]tap to reveal
Solution

It increases. The next-best alternative (renting to tourists) now earns more, so the income given up by renting long-term is higher. Definition first (value of the next-best alternative given up), then the mechanism.

2Laia works 8 h and per hour makes 4 reports or 16 slides. Marc works 8 h and per hour makes 2 reports or 12 slides. Find the opportunity costs, comparative advantage, and draw the joint PPF with reports on the vertical axis.[15]tap to reveal
Solution

Per day: Laia 32 reports or 128 slides; Marc 16 reports or 96 slides.

OC of a slide: Laia 32/128 = 0.25 report; Marc 16/96 = 0.167 report → Marc: slides. OC of a report: Laia 4 slides, Marc 6 slides → Laia: reports.

Joint PPF: max 48 reports, max 224 slides. Starting from 48 reports, Marc switches to slides first (lowest OC of slides) → kink at (96 slides, 32 reports). First segment slope −1/6, second −1/4 (steeper, bowed out).

3A market has five firms with shares 35%, 25%, 20%, 10%, 10%. Calculate the HHI. The two smallest firms merge: new HHI and change? Would the European Commission likely look closer?[8]tap to reveal
Solution

HHI = 1,225 + 625 + 400 + 100 + 100 = 2,450. After merger (35, 25, 20, 20): 1,225 + 625 + 400 + 400 = 2,650, Δ = 200. Post-merger HHI is above 2,000 and Δ ≥ 150, so it falls outside the "unlikely to raise concerns" safe zone → the Commission may examine it. Higher concentration → collusion more likely, higher prices, lower output.

4A gym raises the monthly fee from €40 to €46 and members fall from 500 to 455. Find the PED and the change in revenue. Was it a good decision?[8]tap to reveal
Solution

%ΔP = 6/40 = +15%; %ΔQ = −45/500 = −9% → ε = −0.6, inelastic. Revenue: 40 × 500 = €20,000 → 46 × 455 = €20,930 (+4.65%). Good for revenue; profit cannot be judged without costs.

5PED for a streaming service is −0.5. The company raises the price by 20%. By what percentage does revenue change? Show the revenue areas on a diagram.[8]tap to reveal
Solution

%ΔQ = −0.5 × 20% = −10%. R₁/R₀ = 1.2 × 0.9 = 1.08 → revenue +8%. Diagram: gain rectangle (ΔP × Q₂) at the top is larger than the loss rectangle (P₁ × ΔQ) on the right, because demand is inelastic.

6The PED for train tickets is −0.4 and the cross-price elasticity of train tickets with respect to the price of petrol is +0.3. Petrol rises 20% and train fares rise 5%. Change in train travel?[5]tap to reveal
Solution

Cross effect: 0.3 × 20% = +6%. Own effect: −0.4 × 5% = −2%. Total +4%. Positive XED → trains and petrol (driving) are substitutes.

7"Battery costs keep falling, and more drivers want electric cars after the EU's 2035 ban on new combustion cars." Show the change in the EV market.[7.5]tap to reveal
Solution

Cheaper batteries → lower marginal cost → supply shifts right (P↓, Q↑). Preference for EVs → demand shifts right (P↑, Q↑). Final effect: Q ↑, price uncertain – depends on which shift is larger. Draw both shifts with E₁ and E₂ labelled.

8Qd = 300 − 3p, Qs = −60 + 6p. (a) Equilibrium and welfare. (b) The government sets a minimum price of €50: quantities, CS, PS, DWL. Are producers better off?[20]tap to reveal
Solution

a) 300 − 3p = −60 + 6p → p* = 40, Q* = 180. Demand intercept p = 100; supply intercept p = 10. CS = ½(60)(180) = 5,400; PS = ½(30)(180) = 2,700; TS = 8,100.

b) At €50: Qd = 150, Qs = 240 → excess supply 90; traded Q = 150. Supply price at 150: p = 210/6 = 35.
CS = ½(100 − 50)(150) = 3,750. PS = (50 − 35)(150) + ½(35 − 10)(150) = 2,250 + 1,875 = 4,125. TS = 7,875 → DWL = 225 = ½(50 − 35)(180 − 150).
Producers are better off here (2,700 → 4,125) – assuming only the cheapest 150 units are produced. If firms actually produce 240 and cannot sell 90, they bear that cost.

9Explain, with a diagram, why a price ceiling on rents can make tenants as a group worse off. Under which supply elasticity is this more likely?[10]tap to reveal
Solution

Tenants gain the rectangle (p* − p_max) × Qs on the flats still rented, but lose the triangle between demand and p* for the flats that disappear (Q* − Qs). With elastic supply many flats disappear, so the lost triangle can exceed the gained rectangle (Seminar 3: CS 19.6m → 17.5m). Add: black market, lower quality, less investment.

Where marks get lost

1
Using the new values as the base for %Δ. The course key always divides by the initial price and quantity.
2
Confusing the slope of a linear demand with its elasticity. Slope is constant; ε is elastic at high prices and inelastic at low prices.
3
Treating PS as a triangle when the supply curve starts on the quantity axis (Qs = 100 + 6p → trapezoid).
4
Under a ceiling, computing CS with the old triangle. Read the WTP at Qs from the demand curve and add rectangle + triangle.
5
Saying both P and Q change for certain when both curves shift. One of them is always "?".
6
Mixing up job destruction (L* − Ld) and unemployment (Ls − Ld) in the minimum-wage diagram.
7
Joint PPF: putting the wrong producer first. The good on the axis you move along is made first by whoever has its lowest OC.
8
"Best", "should", "better for society" make a statement normative, even if half of the sentence is a fact.
9
Forgetting fixed costs don't affect supply or producer surplus; PS ≠ profit, max revenue ≠ max profit.
