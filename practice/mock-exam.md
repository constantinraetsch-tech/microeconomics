# Mock midterm

New questions in the style of the 2023 and 2025 papers. Answer on paper first. Solutions are folded – click to open.

**1. [4 marks]** Short-term rental prices in Barcelona rise sharply. What happens to the opportunity cost of a flat owner who rents long-term to students?

<details><summary>Solution</summary>

It **increases**: the next-best alternative (renting to tourists) earns more, so the income given up by renting long-term is higher. Start with the definition (value of the next-best alternative given up).
</details>

**2. [15 marks]** Laia works 8 h; per hour she makes 4 reports or 16 slides. Marc works 8 h; per hour he makes 2 reports or 12 slides. Find the opportunity costs and comparative advantage, and draw the joint PPF (reports vertical).

<details><summary>Solution</summary>

Per day: Laia 32 reports or 128 slides; Marc 16 reports or 96 slides.
OC of a slide: Laia 0.25 report, Marc 0.167 report → **Marc: slides**. OC of a report: Laia 4 slides, Marc 6 → **Laia: reports**.
Joint PPF: max **48 reports**, **224 slides**; Marc switches first → kink at **(96 slides, 32 reports)**; slopes −1/6 then −1/4.
</details>

**3. [8 marks]** Five firms with shares 35, 25, 20, 10, 10 %. HHI? The two smallest merge: new HHI and change? Would the European Commission look closer?

<details><summary>Solution</summary>

HHI = 1,225 + 625 + 400 + 100 + 100 = **2,450**. After: 1,225 + 625 + 400 + 400 = **2,650**, Δ = **200**. HHI > 2,000 with Δ ≥ 150 → outside the safe zone → may be examined. Higher concentration → collusion more likely, higher prices, lower output.
</details>

**4. [8 marks]** A gym raises the fee from €40 to €46; members fall from 500 to 455. PED and revenue change?

<details><summary>Solution</summary>

%ΔP = +15%, %ΔQ = −9% → **ε = −0.6** (inelastic). Revenue €20,000 → **€20,930** (+4.65%).
</details>

**5. [8 marks]** PED = −0.5 and the price rises by 20%. % change in revenue? Show the areas.

<details><summary>Solution</summary>

%ΔQ = −10% → 1.2 × 0.9 = 1.08 → **+8%**. Gain rectangle (ΔP × Q₂) > loss rectangle (P₁ × ΔQ).
</details>

**6. [5 marks]** PED of train tickets −0.4; cross-price elasticity of trains w.r.t. petrol +0.3. Petrol +20%, train fares +5%. Change in train travel?

<details><summary>Solution</summary>

+6% (cross) − 2% (own) = **+4%**. Substitutes.
</details>

**7. [7.5 marks]** "Battery costs keep falling, and more drivers want electric cars after the EU's 2035 ban on new combustion cars." Show the EV market.

<details><summary>Solution</summary>

Supply → right (P↓ Q↑); demand → right (P↑ Q↑). **Q ↑, P uncertain.**
</details>

**8. [20 marks]** Qd = 300 − 3p, Qs = −60 + 6p. (a) Equilibrium and welfare. (b) Minimum price €50: quantities, CS, PS, DWL. Producers better off?

<details><summary>Solution</summary>

a) **p\* = 40, Q\* = 180**. CS = ½(100 − 40)(180) = **5,400**; PS = ½(40 − 10)(180) = **2,700**; TS = 8,100.
b) Qd = 150, Qs = 240 → surplus 90; traded **150**. Supply price at 150 = 35.
CS = ½(50)(150) = **3,750** · PS = 15·150 + ½·25·150 = **4,125** · DWL = ½(15)(30) = **225**. Producers better off (if only 150 are produced).
</details>

**9. [10 marks]** Explain with a diagram why a rent ceiling can make tenants as a group worse off. With which supply elasticity is this more likely?

<details><summary>Solution</summary>

Gain: rectangle (p\* − p_max) × Qs. Loss: triangle for flats that disappear (Q\* − Qs). With **elastic supply** many flats disappear → loss > gain (Seminar 3: 19.6m → 17.5m). Add black market, quality, investment.
</details>
